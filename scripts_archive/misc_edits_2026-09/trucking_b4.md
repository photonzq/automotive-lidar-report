**Is autonomous trucking gaining traction? (late Sep 2026)** [E verdict; facts tagged]
- **Yes, but in three small pockets, all in Texas and the Sun Belt:**
  - Aurora's paid driverless interstate freight;
  - Kodiak's customer-owned driverless trucks in the Permian;
  - Gatik's driverless middle-mile box trucks.
- **Everything else is a pilot or a 2027 plan.** Well under 100 Class 8 trucks run driverless on US public roads.

**Rules and friction:**
- **California:** rules adopted 28 Apr 2026 lift the ban on autonomous vehicles of 10,001 lb and above. Heavy-truck testing with safety drivers began in Aug 2026, and each level of driverless authorization needs 500k test miles [T]. Driverless trucking in California is therefore years away.
- **FMCSA warning devices:** the reflective-triangle rule is handled by rolling three-month waivers (the current one runs to 9 Oct 2026). Aurora has applied for a five-year exemption, and a petition in the 7th Circuit (Sep 2026) challenges the waiver [T/S].

**Economics:** Aurora quotes \$2+ per mile for trucking-as-a-service and \$0.85+ per mile for driver-as-a-service, ≈\$0.4 M of revenue per truck-year [T]. Analysts expect autonomous trucks to undercut human-driven ones around 2028; Goldman Sachs projects ≈25,000 autonomous trucks by 2030 [MR]. These are forecasts, not results.

**What it means for FMCW** [E]:
- **Demand is small and mostly captive.** Assuming 1–2 FirstLight units per Aurora truck (undisclosed), plus Aeva's Daimler programme:
  - 2026: a few hundred units;
  - 2027: ≈1–5k;
  - 2028: ≈3–20k.
- **Against the ToF market** that is under 1% of lidar units even on the upside (Hesai alone plans >4 M in 2026), and much of it is Aurora's in-house FirstLight, not merchant sales.
- **The near-term test** is whether Aurora goes from ≈25 to 200 driverless trucks in Q4 2026.
- **The structural test** is whether any operator shows positive unit economics at hundreds of trucks. Only then do the 2030 truck counts that would make FMCW a business become plausible (§C7b).
